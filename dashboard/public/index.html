Agentic payments

The original problem: letting an agent buy from a stranger, per call, with neither side trusting the other.

🔐

Programmable escrow

Payment locks before delivery and releases only when the response passes an integrity and quality check.

↩️

SLA auto-refund

Miss the deadline or return junk and the agent reclaims its USDC automatically — enforced by the contract, not goodwill.

⭐

Reputation & bonded stake

Every settlement and failure is recorded against the provider, and a breach slashes the USDC they put up.

⚡

Nanopayment tabs

One deposit, then hundreds of off-chain signed vouchers at zero gas, settled in a single transaction.

🛡

Guardian policy

A spending cap keeps the agent autonomous for small buys and escalates anything larger to a human co-signer.

🧠

Trust memory & billing inbox

The agent remembers who burned it, pays invoices from names it trusts and declines the rest, unattended.

⧉

Sell your own services

List a paid endpoint in the marketplace and take the same escrowed, reputation-backed payments the agent does.

⚖

Escrow as a service

Two strangers who have nothing to do with the agent can settle a trade through the same contract.

DeFi — the money the agent works with

A money market, a vault, an AMM and a swap desk, all USDC-denominated, all reachable from your own wallet.

🏦

Lending & borrowing

Supply USDC, EURC, cirBTC or TSRA for yield, or borrow against collateral. Separate borrow and liquidation thresholds leave a buffer instead of putting the two on the same line.

📈

Reactive interest curve

A three-slope kinked curve with a modifier that drifts toward the target utilisation, so rates answer demand rather than waiting for an operator.

🛟

First-loss backstop

Deposit cover that is paid a share of borrower interest and is the first balance a bad debt is written against — priced risk, not a promise.

🏛

Yield vault

Deposit USDC and earn the pool's APR automatically. An 80% liquid floor is a contract constant no admin can lower, so most of it is always redeemable.

🌊

Liquidity pools

Provide to a pool and keep 50% of every swap fee — a floor written into the contract. Withdrawals stay open even while a pool is frozen.

🔁

Swap

Routed through the pools' own reserves, so no external oracle can be moved against you. The rate and both balances are shown before you commit.

◪

App fees, split in public

What the protocol earns is divided between the agent, the pool, the vault and the AMM on a published schedule, and every allocation is on the chart.

🎯

Guarded prices

A price guard bands every mark, a risk oracle marks collateral low and debt high, and an outflow limiter caps how fast the pool can be drained.

TSRA — earned, not sold

A fixed supply released against measured activity, paid to the people carrying the protocol's risk.

🔒

Fixed supply, no mint

The whole supply exists after the constructor returns and there is no function that creates another unit. Fixed by absence, not by policy.

⏳

Released against activity

The emitter sizes each second's release from real supplied and borrowed value, under a hard rate ceiling — so an empty protocol emits nothing.

🎁

Every side is paid

Suppliers, borrowers, backstop depositors and liquidity providers all accrue, and claim to their own wallet whenever they like.

💳

Pays for the agent's work

Buy prepaid credit for the agent's services in USDC, or in TSRA at a discount — the token has a job beyond being held.

Governance — who decides

Token holders steer the rewards, the listings and the treasury. Voting weight is delegated stake, not a balance snapshot anyone can borrow.

🗳

Vote where rewards go

Each epoch the gauge splits emissions across markets by vote. Weight spent is weight committed, and the split is enacted on-chain, not by an operator.

📜

Proposals & timelock

Anything that changes the protocol goes through a proposal, a vote and a delay — long enough for anyone who disagrees to get out first.

📋

Asset registry

What the protocol lists is a vote, and the register keeps the assets it turned down as well as the ones it took.

🤝

Delegation

Tokens carry no weight until they are delegated — to yourself or to somebody who shows up. The panel measures both and offers the fix.

What makes it different

Most agent-payment projects stop at "the agent sends USDC". These are the parts that make a stranger safe to deal with.

Unique

💳 Borrowers — who owes the pool

Every address with debt outstanding, largest first. Health is distance to liquidation, not to the borrow limit — below 1.00 the position can be seized. "Owes" is the face value of the debt; "counts as" is what the pool actually measures it against, which is larger because each asset's liability factor marks a debt up. That second number is the one that decides whether anyone may borrow or withdraw another unit.
AddressOwesCounts as CollateralHealthAssets

🛡️ Backstop — who takes the loss first

A lending pool needs someone standing in front of its depositors, and that someone should be paid for it rather than volunteered. Backstop depositors earn a share of every unit of borrower interest; in exchange, when a position ends up with debt and no collateral, that loss is paid out of the backstop before it reaches supplier balances. Exits are queued for 21 days — capital that can leave the moment a loss becomes visible is not insurance.
AssetCover in the pot Your positionExit queue

⚖️ Liquidation auction — clearing a position at a discovered price

The immediate path repays up to half a debt at a fixed 10% bonus, which is a guess: too much for a position that would clear at 2%, too little for one nobody will touch under 20%. An auction ramps instead — it opens at terms no liquidator would take and improves every second until someone does. It can take the whole debt rather than half, and it can be filled in parts, so several liquidators can clear a position together instead of one needing the entire repayment on hand. The percentage must leave the borrower's health factor between 1.03 and 1.15: below that they are liquidatable again on the next tick of interest, above it more collateral was sold than the problem required.
Percent is of the borrower's debt when starting, and of what is left of the auction when filling. A position with debt and no collateral left cannot be auctioned at all — it is written off with Clear bad debt, which pays it out of the backstop first and only then reduces every supplier's claim pro rata.

🏛 Governance — TSRA decides where the emissions go

            

              Emissions are the protocol's only recurring outflow, so directing them is the decision with
              real money attached. TSRA holders vote on where rewards are pointed; a passed proposal makes
              the calls it carries. Weight is a snapshot taken the block before a proposal opened, so
              buying tokens after seeing the question buys no say in it — and tokens you hold carry no
              weight until you delegate them, which is why a pool or an exchange cannot vote with yours.

Emission rates — what each market pays

            

              Operator only. A rate is in reward units per second across every share on that side, so the
              APY a supplier sees falls as more of them arrive. Leave the end date empty to run
              indefinitely; set one and the stream stops on its own, so a campaign cannot outlive the
              intention behind it because nobody remembered to switch it off.

            Return sends the app's own tokens to each holder; Migrate re-creates their
            position in the replacement contract, also paid by the app. Neither moves a user's existing
            position — no contract here has a function that can, by design — so their claim on the old
            contract survives either way.
